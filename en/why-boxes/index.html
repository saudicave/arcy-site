E-103

Why an AI architect draws boxes, and what it takes to stop

Draft article: not published or announced; it waits for the guard's and the owner's verdict.

Asked for a house, our agent delivered a box, and our own checks let it through. This is where the box comes from, what we change first, and why the result is not yet measured.

When the owner of ARCY judged our first paid measurement, the record called the buildings boxes in an empty desert. A reviewer's note on the house is more exact: the programme is solved, with the majlis by the entrance and away from the family hall, and the architecture is absent.

On 24 November 2026 we studied why. The study read two session records, their pictures and the library code, with no code changed and no paid call; what follows comes from it.

The box is decided in the first program

The request was one sentence: a two-storey house, 10 × 12 metres, majlis, living room and kitchen below, three bedrooms above.

The agent's first building program is a list of room rectangles: seven that fill the footprint on the ground floor, six that fill it again above. The form is fixed there; what follows repairs doors and makes drawings.

The study names the reason: the box is the cheapest path through the product. The call that makes rooms tiles rectangles, one call a storey, so a box is its natural output. And the building guide's own example is a house of 12 × 10 metres with nearly the same rooms: the agent's program is that example re-sized.

The box is the cheapest path through the product.

A design guide that starts from the mass exists, and was never opened: the request said "build", and that word leads to the building guide.

The better path existed, and stopped at a diagram

ARCY's massing library makes and measures courts, L forms, stepped sections. In a second session the agent used it for three ideas for a family house in Riyadh: a ring around a court, a street wing with a one-storey arm, and a compact block of two equal floors. The measures told them apart correctly.

There the path ended: nothing turned a mass into a building.

The study's author tested this by hand, writing the court house with the calls of that day. Four programs gave one usable result; three were refused; and the usable one was measured wrongly, because a court enclosed by rooms was not read as a court. A stepped court house cost five or six programs where a box cost one.

An earlier plan had named this gap; of the eleven building blocks it listed to carry a mass into a model, none existed.

Our own measures waved the box through

The comparison ranked the box first. Of the three ideas, the compact block scored 0.647 and the court house 0.429. The score sums seven unweighted objectives; three reward compactness directly. Privacy, the reason for a court in Riyadh and the critic's reason, is not measured. The critic preferred the court; the box still went forward as one of two ideas to develop.

The check named the box and accepted a sentence. In the house session the check reported the building plain, 6 of 6, three times, and its note offered a way out: name plainness as the decision. The agent did, in one sentence of its log.

The gate and the critic came too late. The concept gate fired after the building was finished, and filling one field passed it. The critic, asked last, was refused: the money left was reserved for the final answer.

Guidance against the box was given three times in prose and crowded out each time. More words will not change that; a cheaper correct program will.

More words will not change that; a cheaper correct program will.

What we change first

The owner chose the study's first three changes to begin the cycle.

  1. The bridge. One library call that turns a mass into the building's shell: walls around each storey's outline, courts included; floors; roofs with their parapets; terraces; the boundary wall. It holds no design decision: every figure comes from the mass the agent wrote. This change has since been built and passes its own tests on recorded masses, and a court of walls is now read as a court. No paid run has used it yet.
  2. The route and the example. The guides are to send any new building to the design guide, and the building guide's example is to take its outline from a mass. The standing instructions get shorter. Not made yet.
  3. The instruments. The comparison stops leading with a sum; a gate stops once, whatever is filled; the concept gate sits where a building is made; plainness counts as a decision only when recorded where the person can see it. Not made yet.

What we do not know

  • Whether it works. No paid run has yet taken this method from masses to a building. It is reasoned from two sessions, older attempts and one free trial.
  • Whether "not a box" is enough. Among four earlier villa attempts, the one that was not a box scored 4.38 of 10 from a blind juror, against 2.38 for the first: competent but ordinary, in the record's words. The owner's 3 of 4 also needs a facade with depth and order, the drawings and the context, which are other cycles.
  • How hard it is. Our plan calls this the hardest cycle.

What we do next

The study's order puts free steps first: the bridge's tests, now passed; a small paid check that "build" takes the mass-first route; one brief judged by the owner; four more only if it shows an idea, the last being the original request word for word.

The owner answers one question by eye: is there an idea in the mass, the plan and the section? If the design does not reach 3 after two cycles, the plan shows the owner the alternatives before more is spent. Under the plan, each run is measured before and after, and its numbers go into the results record.
