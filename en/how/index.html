  1. A-210

    How ARCY works

    You watch it work, you steer it, and nothing of yours changes without your yes.

  2. A-210.2

    It draws where you can see it.

    The agent has its own named cursor on the shared canvas. Where the cursor is, the work is.

  3. A-210.3

    Its plan ticks itself.

    The plan is a list of steps, each one done, under way or still to come, and it marks them as it goes.

  4. A-210.4

    It shows what it understood, before it designs.

    The plot, the storeys, the court, the character: it says them back to you and asks whether it has them right.

  5. A-210.5

    It asks by showing, not by making you type.

    Choices to pick from, a slider on what can be tuned, two options side by side, and a pointer at the very wall it is asking about. It composes the view the moment needs, from parts it trusts.

  6. A-210.6

    Every change to your file is a proposal.

    You accept it or you reject it. Until you accept, your file is as you left it.

  7. A-210.7

    It checks its own work before it says done.

    A finished sheet is read back against the model it was drawn from, figure by figure.

  8. A-210.8

    Several agents work at once, each in its own colour.

    Sub-agents work beside it in parallel, and you can tell at a glance who is doing what.

  9. A-210.9

    You steer it while it works.

    Point at a thing, draw over it, or type a line. It turns to what you showed it.

Abstract drawing, not an ARCY output.

What these drawings are

Abstract drawings of how the product behaves. The interface is being rebuilt, so we show no picture of it here.

What this page does not say
